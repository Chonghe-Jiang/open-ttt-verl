Test-time training

Learning to evolve algorithms in thought space

We separate deciding what idea to try from deciding how to implement it. A small guidance policy writes the next research proposal; a large frozen executor implements it as a complete program; every program earns one exact score. Test-time reinforcement learning then updates only the small policy over ideas. One run evolves a Polyomino Packing solver from 27.81 to 91.89 — above the reported multi-agent and human references on the same evaluator.

Method

Two models, two spaces

The guidance model reads a compact summary of the current algorithm and proposes a direction for its evolution — a new mechanism, representation, or search strategy. The execution model receives that guidance together with the complete parent program, realizes the change, and returns a summary of what actually changed. Guidance and summary form an evolving state in thought space; the full artifact stays lossless in execution space, so abstraction never silently deletes a working mechanism.

The trained guidance model behaves like a world model for algorithm evolution: it treats each returned summary as a state, proposes a plausible transition, and learns from the score that comes back. Cumulative progress requires all three links to hold at once — the guidance must be novel enough to change behavior, the implementation complete enough to preserve the parent's strengths, and the summary clear enough to serve as the next state.

Results

One trajectory, from seed to frontier

Polyomino Packing (FrontierCS problem 0) asks a single program to pack irregular pieces into a minimal rectangle across 70 hidden cases. Every idea must survive as one complete solver; every solver receives an exact score. The final Guidance-TTT artifact — a 2,759-line C++ solver discovered at step 27 — scores 91.89, above the reported CORAL four-agent system and the released human reference on the same evaluator.

  • Steps 0–3Discover the topology. Row packing becomes symmetry-aware skyline search with annealing — 27.81 → 87.56.
  • Steps 4–12Adapt the search. Width priors, conflict prediction, and multi-modal restarts — to 90.84.
  • Steps 13–20Close the feedback loop. Aspect, density, and fragmentation become live control signals — to 91.76.
  • Steps 21–27Predict residual space. Gap depth becomes shape compatibility, then multi-step look-ahead — to 91.89.
  • Steps 28–31Saturate. 445 further valid programs, no frontier gain.

The kind of improvement shifts over the run: first a new representation, then operationalized diagnostics, then a mechanism that exploits them. The winning step-27 mutation came from one sentence of guidance — "simulate placing multiple pieces sequentially in predicted optimal positions" — realized as a cheap three-step residual-rectangle forecast inside the placement score.

Evidence

One matrix of controls

Every baseline and ablation varies one of two things: the strategic layer above the executor, or the executor itself. Arranged on those two axes, the runs stop being scattered experiments. Reading down a column asks what the thought-space channel adds under a fixed executor; reading across the bottom row asks what executor capability is worth under a fixed, trained strategist.

  1. The thought-space channel does real work before any learning.

    Under GPT-OSS-120B, adding a frozen strategist moves 69.92 to 80.87. Under GLM-5.2, frozen guidance reaches 84.85 versus 78.36 for direct OpenEvolve. Separating strategy from implementation helps even when the strategist never updates.

  2. Learning the strategist is consistent with a further gain.

    With GLM-5.2 execution and the same 3,840-candidate search budget, frozen Qwen3-8B guidance reaches 84.85 while Guidance-TTT reaches 91.89. This matched-budget pair is substantially stronger evidence than the earlier cross-budget comparison, although repeated seeds are still needed for a causal estimate.

  3. Executor capability is multiplicative, not additive.

    Without a strategist, stronger executors stall near 80 (top two rows). With the same trained 8B strategist, they reach 85.32 and 91.89 (bottom row) — the gain from the strategic layer grows with the executor that realizes it.

  4. Actor scale is conditional.

    Tripling the strategist to 27B changes nothing (80.18 vs 80.20 under GPT-OSS-120B), and neither does a longer reasoning window (32K vs 48K context). Once a small actor can name the useful mechanisms, the bottleneck sits downstream, in execution fidelity.

What 91.89 does not prove. The matched GLM-5.2 comparison now separates frozen guidance (84.85) from trained guidance (91.89) at the same sample count. It remains a single-run comparison: repeated seeds are needed to distinguish a stable learning effect from trajectory-level search variance, and the 300-step OpenEvolve run is not budget-matched.
Optimization

A max-seeking update over ideas

Executor calls and verifier runs are expensive; the learned object is a small policy over guidance messages. Each update selects eight archived states by PUCT, samples sixteen guidance messages per state, executes and verifies all 128 candidates, and applies a critic-free REINFORCE step to guidance tokens only.

xi = exp[β(ri − rmax)] → Ai = xi / meanj≠i(xj) − 1 → L = −mean(ρi,t Ai log πθ)

Average-reward objectives underweight the event that matters most in discovery: one candidate breaking the frontier. The entropic advantage re-weights each group exponentially, with β chosen per group so that DKL(qβ ‖ Uniform) = log 2 — flat groups get β = 0 and exert no pressure. Leave-one-out baselines prevent a strong sample from softening its own comparison; importance ratios ρ and a KL penalty to the reference policy keep off-policy credit usable and drift bounded.

Limitations

Why the search digs in one direction

The plateau after step 27 is not a failure of execution — post-best step means stay between 88.26 and 90.80. It is an on-policy feedback loop: reward promotes a lineage into the archive, PUCT concentrates rollouts on its descendants, and the policy is updated on that increasingly local distribution. Each successful local update makes a jump to a different algorithmic world less likely. The policy doesn't just learn which action worked; it reshapes the future data it will learn from.

A genuinely different lineage may need to begin by breaking something that works — accepting a temporary score debt while a new representation assembles its own mechanisms. That points at agentic test-time training: a system that learns not only the next research move but when to stop exploiting, launch a speculative branch, protect it through early losses, and judge it on multi-step return. The learned object stops being a guidance message and becomes a policy over research programs.
