Learning to evolve algorithms in thought space

Guidance-TTT separates deciding what idea to try from deciding how to implement it. Test-time training then improves the policy over research moves, while exact artifacts and semantic summaries carry discoveries forward.

1

Evolve in thought space

Guidance-TTT gives two models different cognitive roles. One proposes the next direction for evolution; the other realizes it and explains what changed relative to the parent. A guidance message moves forward, and an implementation-aware summary comes back. Together, they form an evolving state in thought space.

Guidance describes a direction for evolution. Summary records how the implementation changed from its parent and turns that realized transition into the next state of thought.
The guidance model proposes the next transition

It reads a compact design state and proposes how the current artifact should evolve through a new mechanism, representation, or search direction.

The execution model realizes and reports the transition

It edits the complete parent artifact, preserves its working dependencies, and summarizes what changed in the implementation.

The verifier grounds the transition

It determines whether the proposed direction became a valid, behavior-changing, score-improving artifact.

Core insight The trained guidance model functions as a world model for algorithm evolution.

It treats the returned summary as a state, proposes a plausible transition, and learns from the verifier consequence. This creates a new two-agent interaction mechanism: one model learns which evolution directions are promising, while the other grounds each direction in a complete implementation and returns a parent-relative account of what actually changed.

Next: make the abstraction concrete A case study asks whether the two thought-space messages survive a long executable trajectory.

We next follow one complete discovery run from a weak seed to a strong final solution. The key question is not only whether the score rises, but whether guidance stays novel, execution stays complete, and each returned summary remains clear enough to support the next experiment.

2

A case study in Polyomino Packing

Polyomino Packing gives the loop a hard executable test: ideas must become one complete solution, every solution receives an exact score, and small strategic changes can have measurable geometric consequences.

The task Pack irregular pieces tightly

Choose orientations, orderings, placements, and search strategy so every piece fits inside a small enclosing rectangle.

The artifact One complete solver

Each proposal must become a valid end-to-end program, not a prose answer or a collection of hand-selected partial solutions.

The feedback 70-case verifier

The same submitted solver is executed on every case; the evaluator aggregates packing density into a 0–100 score.

Why it is useful Dense but unforgiving

Reward is informative, while validity, geometry, search quality, and implementation fidelity must all hold simultaneously.

Search architecture

How the solver turns shapes into a dense board

The result is not one greedy rule. It establishes feasible packings, explores board geometry, and then spends the remaining runtime refining fragmentation and placement order.

01 Normalize geometry

Generate unique reflected and rotated orientations, then compute compactness, symmetry, and fitting features.

02 Sweep board widths

Estimate useful aspect ratios and test widths with several fixed piece orderings to establish strong baselines.

03 Build by skyline

Place oriented pieces using height, waste, slope, gap compatibility, and placement-pressure signals.

04 Refine and reboot

Combine dynamic ordering, bias perturbations, simulated annealing, width moves, and periodic restarts.

05 · Step-27 mutation Forecast residual space

Simulate three hypothetical bounding-box insertions and favor residual rectangles that remain fillable.

Parent · 91.8366 Immediate gap prediction

The parent already combined skyline placement, aspect-aware width exploration, gap classification, dynamic fragmentation modes, multiple orderings, and annealed reconfiguration. Its predictive score evaluated the next placement but not a short placement sequence.

GLM child · 91.8907 Three-step residual forecasting

The child repeatedly selects the largest residual rectangle, tests its best-fitting orientation dimensions, splits the rectangle, and scores fill ratio plus continued usability. This is an inexpensive bounding-box forecast rather than exact combinatorial look-ahead: simulated pieces are not removed from the remaining multiset.

“Instead of evaluating only immediate residual spaces after a single placement, simulate placing multiple pieces sequentially in predicted optimal positions.”

Why this trajectory accumulates instead of repeatedly restarting

The most important pattern is the shift in the kind of improvement. Steps 1–3 change the search topology. Steps 4–20 turn that topology into a state-dependent controller. Steps 21–27 change the information used by the controller from immediate geometry to predicted future geometry. This is what a productive research trajectory looks like: new representation, operationalized diagnostics, then a mechanism that exploits those diagnostics.

What the score does—and does not—prove 91.89 validates the complete system, not the isolated effect of actor learning.

The observed frontier combines a learned actor, PUCT archive selection, best-of-16 execution sampling, GLM-5.2’s implementation prior, and verifier selection. It proves that the hierarchy can produce and explain a strong solver. The matched-budget frozen-actor GLM run reaches 84.85 versus 91.89 with training, providing direct evidence consistent with a learning benefit; repeated seeds are still required to estimate that benefit causally.

3

The science of Guidance-TTT

A successful hierarchy needs two layers to work together. The communication layer must preserve novelty, implementation completeness, and summary clarity. The optimization layer must turn expensive executor-and-verifier outcomes into an efficient update of the policy over ideas.

Layer 1 · Communication and realization

All three links in the semantic loop must be strong

The summary channel compresses the current algorithm into mechanisms, measured outcomes, unresolved bottlenecks, and a proposed direction. It also creates the unit to which verifier credit is assigned. Meanwhile, the full parent program bypasses that bottleneck and goes directly to the executor. This prevents abstraction from silently deleting a working mechanism.

The case study suggests a simple diagnostic product. It is not an independence assumption; it is a way to ask which link is preventing a promising thought from becoming cumulative progress.

Quality of evolution ≈ novel guidance × complete implementation × clear summary

Guidance Does the idea add behavioral leverage?

A useful proposal targets a real bottleneck or introduces a new mechanism—not merely a new description.

Implementation Does the idea survive as a complete artifact?

The executor must realize the requested change while preserving every dependency that made the parent work.

Summary Does the outcome become a clear next state?

The return message must separate mechanism, measured result, unresolved hypothesis, and next bottleneck.

Read the ladder as three increasingly strong claims

The direct-mutation run shows that archive reuse and repeated code edits are not enough to reproduce the stronger trajectories. Repeated seeds remain necessary.

Why summary-only is the default

Historical runs varied whether the guidance actor saw a compact summary or an artifact-heavy state containing code-related material. Results were non-monotone: the 8B summary-only run reached 83.13 versus 81.97 for its alternative, while the 14B ordering reversed at 80.40 versus 82.53. Those runs also differed in sample count, horizon, and interruption mode, so they do not identify a universally superior serialization.

Summary-only is the default for a more fundamental reason. The actor’s scientific role is to maintain a compact, causally meaningful design state. The executor’s engineering role is to preserve every exact dependency in the program. Giving both models the same state erases that division of cognitive labor and makes policy credit increasingly depend on source length.

Communication succeeds only when the proposal is novel, the implementation is complete, and the returned summary is clear. A weak link in any one of the three prevents cumulative evolution.

Empirical law 1 Scale follows the active bottleneck

A larger strategist has little leverage after a smaller actor can already name the useful mechanisms and execution fidelity becomes limiting.

Empirical law 2 Executor scale is multiplicative

The executor affects validity, fidelity, code repair, and missing implementation details, changing how often every strategic proposal becomes reward.

Empirical law 3 Semantic density beats raw context

Longer reasoning can bundle coupled edits and dilute causal state. Relevant bits per verified experiment matter more than maximum token count.

Empirical law 4 Test-time compute changes regime

Early budget discovers representations, middle budget refines a controller, and late budget often deepens a saturated branch.

Law 1: actor scale is conditional, not monotone

Under the same GPT-OSS-120B executor and 8 × 16 sampling shape, the high-reasoning 8B and 27B guidance runs are essentially tied at 80.18 and 80.20. Earlier 8B/14B interface experiments were non-monotone as well: their best scores were 81.97 and 83.13 for the two 8B runs, versus 82.53 and 80.40 for the two 14B runs. A larger strategist cannot help if the state omits the decisive fact or the executor cannot realize the proposed mutation.

This is a bottleneck statement, not a claim that strategic capacity is universally irrelevant. Once an 8B model can express “multi-width skyline,” “fragmentation-aware control,” or “residual look-ahead,” more parameters help only if they increase the probability of identifying a better bottleneck. If the summary does not expose that bottleneck—or if the executor cannot compile it into behavior—the larger actor produces richer language without a frontier shift.

Law 2: execution capability changes the value of every idea

Keeping an 8B guidance actor and changing the executor shifts the observed frontier much more: first to 85.32 with GPT-5.4, then to 91.89 with GLM-5.2. The executor is not a passive renderer. It contributes algorithmic priors, coordinates cross-cutting edits, repairs code, and preserves a growing solver. The current bottleneck therefore sits downstream of strategic fluency.

This effect is multiplicative. Better execution does not merely improve the final formatting stage; it increases the chance that every useful strategic mutation survives as valid, faithful behavior. It may also supply algorithmic details absent from the summary. That is why executor capability can move both the speed of early discovery and the height of the late frontier.

The new GLM-5.2 ladder sharpens this interpretation: direct OpenEvolve reaches 78.3591 after 300 steps, frozen Qwen3-8B guidance reaches 84.85, and trained guidance reaches 91.89. The frozen and trained runs use the same 30-step, 128-rollout-per-step candidate budget. Local CORAL remains a complementary topology control: one coding agent reaches 80.36 with GPT-5.4 and 80.67 with GLM-5.2, while two GPT-5.4 agents reach 83.80.

Law 3: more context is not more causal state

The 8B high-reasoning run uses a 32K total context with a 16.5K first phase; the 27B run uses 48K with a 30K first phase. Their frontiers are nearly identical. Actor size and horizon differ, so this is not a clean context ablation, but it rejects the simple story that more reasoning room automatically unlocks stronger evolution.

Long guidance can describe many coupled modifications, making it harder to identify which semantic action caused the reward. Long generated code increases latency, transport failures, and the implementation state future rollouts must preserve. The useful quantity is closer to causally relevant semantic bits per verified execution than raw tokens.

Law 4: compute moves from discovery to exploitation

In the GLM run, the winning lineage reaches 87.56 by step 3, takes another 24 updates to reach 91.89, then receives four more complete updates without improving. Early compute buys a new representation. Middle compute buys feedback control and prediction. Late compute buys 445 valid variations inside the same mature algorithm family.

This means the return to test-time compute depends on search topology. More samples are valuable while the system can enter a new thought-space region. Once the ontology saturates, the same budget scales exploitation unless an explicit branching or reset mechanism changes what can be proposed.

Layer 2 · Efficient test-time learning

Turn expensive executable trials into a focused actor update

Once communication is well formed, the second problem is optimization. Executor calls and verifier runs are expensive, while the learned object is a compact guidance policy. The RL algorithm should spend those outcomes on discovering rare improvements—not on training the executor or averaging away the high-reward tail.

The current implementation combines best-child PUCT state selection, an adaptive entropic leave-one-out advantage, and a critic-free, importance-sampled REINFORCE update. Only guidance tokens receive gradients.

A max-seeking objective for discovery

Standard average-reward learning can underweight the event that matters most in open-ended discovery: one candidate breaks the current frontier. Guidance-TTT converts group rewards into exponential weights. The inverse temperature is chosen adaptively so the induced reward distribution satisfies DKL(qβ ‖ Uniform) = log 2; β is zero for a constant-reward group, so a flat group receives no artificial preference.

xi = exp[β(ri − rmax)] → Ai = xi / meanj≠i(xj) − 1 → L = −mean(ρi,tAi log πθ)

β is selected by a fixed entropy/KL target. Each sample is excluded from its own normalizer. ρ corrects the rollout-policy / learner-policy mismatch before reward is assigned to valid guidance tokens.

PUCT reuses expensive history.

Previously evaluated summaries are not discarded. Search statistics decide where another executor call has the highest strategic value.

Adaptive entropy preserves discovery bias.

The reward scale can change across parents and stages; choosing β from the group avoids a fixed temperature that is too flat or too brittle.

Leave-one-out credit avoids self-normalization.

A strong sample cannot make its own baseline easier. Its advantage is measured against the other experiments launched from the same state.

Importance sampling keeps rollout credit usable.

The actor may change between generation and learning. Token-level probability ratios align the update, while a reference KL term limits drift.

What “efficient” means here

Efficiency comes from training only the smaller strategic policy, retaining executor and verifier outputs in a reusable tree, and concentrating credit on frontier-moving guidance. The executor does not need gradients; the complete artifact does not need to be regenerated from summary memory; and flat groups do not create arbitrary policy pressure. A reference-policy KL penalty with coefficient 0.05 provides an additional stability constraint.

Important qualification Focused credit assignment is not the same as proven end-to-end sample efficiency.

The primary configuration still evaluates 128 executions per update. A matched comparison against frozen guidance, direct solution-level TTT, and alternative group objectives is required to quantify verifier calls, generated tokens, wall-clock time, and frontier area under a fixed budget.

Layer 1 determines whether an idea survives communication. Layer 2 determines whether expensive evidence changes the future distribution of ideas.
4

Rethink: Why test-time training digs in one direction

Guidance-TTT’s coherence is also a source of path dependence. One guidance policy is repeatedly updated on-policy from states produced by its own recent behavior. Once a direction earns reward, the policy visits more descendants of that direction and learns from them again. A jump to a different algorithmic world becomes less likely with every successful local update.

Reward One direction wins

A mechanism improves and its state enters the frontier.

On-policy data Its descendants dominate

New rollouts increasingly begin inside the winning ontology.

Update The policy moves toward them

Reward raises the probability of nearby evolution directions.

Lock-in Depth replaces a jump

A distant idea must survive an initial loss before it can mature.

The plateau is an on-policy feedback loop

The post-best candidates are mostly valid and strong. Their step means remain between 88.26 and 90.80. The system has not forgotten how to execute; it is sampling a mature basin whose local variations no longer produce verified gain. More test-time compute in this regime scales exploitation rather than discovery.

The mechanism is self-reinforcing. Reward promotes a lineage into the library. PUCT allocates more rollouts around its descendants. The guidance policy is then updated on this increasingly local state distribution, which makes related transitions more probable in the next round. The policy does not merely learn which action worked. It also changes the future data from which it will learn.

The frozen-guidance run shows that archive selection alone can already create path dependence. After reaching 80.87 at step 38, it keeps generating substantive guidance from 295 selected parents without improving the frontier. The learned setting adds a second feedback loop: search concentrates the data, then on-policy learning concentrates the policy. Directional deepening is therefore not simple model collapse or weak execution. It is a natural consequence of coupling memory, selection, and one continually updated policy around the current frontier.

When should the policy leave its own frontier?

Refinement is rational while local semantic depth still produces frontier gain. Once parent-improvement probability flattens, however, immediate reward becomes a poor test for genuinely different ideas. Structural change can require deleting a successful mechanism, replacing its representation, or rebuilding the controller around a different state. The first complete version may be worse even when the new lineage has a higher long-run ceiling.

A stronger system therefore needs two timescales. Most compute can refine the current frontier, while a protected budget launches ontology-changing branches and evaluates them over several transitions. Those branches must be allowed to incur short-term score debt. Otherwise on-policy updates will prune exactly the valley-crossing behavior needed to discover a new algorithm family.

What to build next

A genuine jump may need to begin by breaking something that currently works. The system tries a different representation, accepts an initial score drop, and gives the new branch enough time to assemble its own mechanisms before comparing ceilings. Once a system can decide when to stop exploiting, launch such a branch, preserve it through temporary failure, and judge its long-horizon return, it is approaching agentic TTT. The learned object is no longer only the next guidance message; it is a policy over multi-step research programs.

  1. Allow strategic regressions.

    Give speculative branches a score-debt budget and a fixed horizon before pruning them. A temporarily worse artifact may be the entry point to a better algorithm family.

  2. Train a policy over research modes.

    Let a meta-policy choose among refine, branch, reset, preserve, and merge instead of forcing one guidance policy to optimize every step on the current frontier.

  3. Evaluate trajectories, not only mutations.

    Assign delayed credit to a coherent sequence of experiments when its long-horizon return exceeds the frontier, even if its first transitions lose reward.

  4. Preserve independent thought worlds.

    Maintain diverse libraries or policies whose summaries use different representations, then compare and merge them only after each has had enough compute to mature.

Guidance-TTT learns how to improve inside the current thought world. Agentic TTT must also learn when to leave it, absorb a temporary loss, and construct a new one.
